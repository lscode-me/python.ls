# Python for Bioinformatics - Quiz

Test your knowledge of bioinformatics concepts and Python applications.

---

## Question 1: DNA Bases

Which of the following is NOT a valid DNA nucleotide?

A) Adenine (A)  
B) Uracil (U)  
C) Guanine (G)  
D) Cytosine (C)  

**Correct Answer: B** (Uracil is found in RNA, not DNA)

---

## Question 2: Genetic Code

How many nucleotides make up a codon?

A) 2  
B) 3  
C) 4  
D) 6  

**Correct Answer: B**

---

## Question 3: GC Content

Why is GC content important in molecular biology?

A) It determines the protein size  
B) It affects DNA stability (G-C has 3 hydrogen bonds vs A-T's 2)  
C) It indicates the number of genes  
D) It has no biological significance  

**Correct Answer: B**

---

## Question 4: FASTA Format

What character starts a header line in FASTA format?

A) `@`  
B) `#`  
C) `>`  
D) `*`  

**Correct Answer: C**

---

## Question 5: Quality Scores

In FASTQ format, what does a Phred score of Q30 represent?

A) 30% accuracy  
B) 99% accuracy  
C) 99.9% accuracy  
D) 100% accuracy  

**Correct Answer: C**

---

## Question 6: Sequence Alignment

What is the purpose of sequence alignment?

A) To make sequences the same length  
B) To identify regions of similarity and evolutionary relationships  
C) To translate DNA to protein  
D) To remove errors from sequences  

**Correct Answer: B**

---

## Question 7: Open Reading Frame (ORF)

What defines an Open Reading Frame?

A) Any sequence of DNA  
B) A sequence between a start codon (ATG) and a stop codon  
C) A sequence that codes for RNA  
D) The first 100 nucleotides of a gene  

**Correct Answer: B**

---

## Question 8: BioPython Seq Object

How do you get the reverse complement of a DNA sequence using BioPython?

A) `seq.reverse()`  
B) `seq.complement()`  
C) `seq.reverse_complement()`  
D) `seq.flip()`  

**Correct Answer: C**

---

## Question 9: Stop Codons

Which of the following is a stop codon?

A) ATG  
B) GCG  
C) TAG  
D) CCG  

**Correct Answer: C**

---

## Question 10: Multiple Sequence Alignment

What is the primary use of Multiple Sequence Alignment (MSA)?

A) To sequence DNA faster  
B) To identify conserved regions across multiple species  
C) To translate proteins  
D) To compress sequence data  

**Correct Answer: B**
